# Overdue Invoice Policy

## Purpose

This policy defines how overdue invoices should be identified and how the Finance Customer Support Agent should respond to customer questions concerning overdue balances.

## Definition of an Overdue Invoice

An invoice is considered overdue when:

1. The invoice has an outstanding balance.
2. The invoice due date has passed.

The Finance Customer Support Agent must rely on the invoice due date and outstanding balance recorded in the finance system.

## Aging Information

The finance system may classify outstanding invoices into aging buckets such as:

- Current
- 1-30 Days
- 31-60 Days
- 61-90 Days
- 90+ Days

The aging bucket stored in the finance system should be treated as the authoritative aging classification for the invoice.

The agent must not change or invent an aging bucket.

## Partially Paid Invoices

An invoice may have a partial payment.

In such cases:

- The original invoice amount remains the invoice amount.
- The amount already received is the amount paid.
- The remaining amount is the outstanding balance.

The agent must use the outstanding amount recorded in the finance system.

## Fully Paid Invoices

An invoice with no remaining outstanding balance should not be described as an unpaid invoice.

The agent must use the payment status recorded in the finance system when available.

## Customer Questions About Overdue Amounts

When a customer asks how much is overdue, the agent should:

1. Identify the correct customer.
2. Retrieve the customer's financial records.
3. Identify invoices with outstanding balances.
4. Use the recorded aging information.
5. Provide the relevant overdue amount.
6. Avoid making unsupported assumptions.

## Questions About Collection Actions

The Finance Customer Support Agent may explain the approved collection process when that information is available in the company's finance policies.

The agent must not promise a collection action, payment extension, waiver, discount, or penalty unless such action is explicitly supported by an approved policy or authorized customer-specific record.

## Invoice Disputes

If a customer disputes an invoice, the agent should provide the available invoice information and follow the approved invoice-dispute procedure.

The agent must not declare an invoice incorrect solely because a customer disputes it.

## Insufficient Information

If the available finance records and approved policy documents do not contain enough information to answer a question reliably, the agent must not guess.

The response should clearly state that the requested information cannot be confirmed from the available records.

## Response Rules

The Finance Customer Support Agent must:

1. Use financial database records for customer-specific financial facts.
2. Use approved policy documents for general finance-policy guidance.
3. Never invent balances, due dates, aging classifications, collection actions, or policy exceptions.
4. Clearly distinguish financial facts from general policy guidance.
5. Avoid making commitments that are not supported by approved company information.
